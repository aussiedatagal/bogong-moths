Year of the Bogong

This spring, swarms of endangered Bogong moths turned up in homes and gardens across south-east Australia.

Each year, nature lovers share their photos on the iNaturalist app, and scientists use them as crowdsourced field work, mostly to map where animals and plants live.

Still endangered

The drought from 2017 crashed the population. A 2021 study found that mountain caves which once held hundreds of thousands of Bogongs were down to a handful of moths. The IUCN listed the Bogong as endangered that same year.

This spring's swarms are a hopeful sign, but nobody has counted how many moths made it.

If Bogongs turn up at your place, Zoos Victoria's Marissa Parrott suggests switching off outdoor lights, keeping cats inside and gently ushering any moths back out. They won't stay long. They have mountains to get to.
